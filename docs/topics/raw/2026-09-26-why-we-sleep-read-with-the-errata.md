## The critique, fetched in full

Alexey Guzey, "Matthew Walker's 'Why We Sleep' Is Riddled with Scientific and Factual Errors"
https://guzey.com/books/why-we-sleep/  (published 2019-11)

Documented items used in the card, each stated as claim vs. what the essay establishes:

1. WHO "sleep loss epidemic" — no such WHO document exists. The cited source (a National Geographic
   documentary) does not mention the WHO. CDC used "epidemic" c.2010-2015, changed to "problem"
   years before the book.
2. "Sleeping under six or seven hours ... more than doubling your risk of cancer" — a 2018
   systematic review, 1.55M participants and 86,201 cancer cases, found neither short nor long
   sleep associated with increased cancer risk.
3. "The shorter your sleep, the shorter your life span" — meta-analyses show a U shape, lowest
   mortality near 7 hours. The Encyclopedia of Sleep, which Walker cites, says the short-sleep /
   short-life expectation "is not supported by the existing literature".
4. A 2014 sports-injury graph: the original 5-hour column sat LOWER than the 6-hour one, against a
   linear story. Walker's reproduction removed the 5-hour column.
5. National Sleep Foundation recommends 7-9 hours, not a fixed 8; WHO publishes no sleep
   recommendation; about one third, not two thirds, sleep under 7 hours.
6. Fatal Familial Insomnia is a prion neurodegenerative disease; patients retain sleep cycles (one
   case documented cycling to month 23) and die of neural degeneration, not of sleeplessness.

The essay explicitly does NOT claim sleep is unimportant, that sleep quality does not matter, that
nobody needs eight hours, or that sleep research is worthless. Its claim is about accuracy.

## Aftermath, from secondary reporting only

Andrew Gelman's blog (statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu, posts of 2019-11-18 and 2019-11-24) reports
that Walker mentioned issuing a corrected second edition, that his public response addressed
"questions from readers" rather than Guzey's points item by item, and that UC Berkeley's official
response characterised the problems as "minor". Walker's and Berkeley's own documents were NOT
opened.

## Stated as inference, not fact

That the Korean edition (2019-02) predates both the essay (2019-11) and the corrected edition, and
therefore probably carries the uncorrected claims. The Korean text was not compared.
