NOTES ON SOURCING (2026-09-25)

The grammatical analysis in the item is mine. The plot context — that Sethe sent her children
ahead to Baby Suggs in Cincinnati while she stayed behind at Sweet Home, that Howard and Buglar
are her two sons, and that the infant she was still nursing is the daughter the novel is named
for — is from memory of the novel, NOT checked against a copy, and the item flags it as such. If
any of it is wrong the grammatical reading still stands, because the reading rests on the sentence
and not on the plot.

What the item argues, as its own contribution:
 1. That the negative past perfect plus `when` is a way of DATING an event by a bodily or
    operational state rather than by a clock, and that this is the transferable move.
 2. That the construction also carries causation without asserting it — "I hadn't stopped X when Y"
    implies the state is why Y went as it did, while remaining, on its face, only a statement about
    timing.
 3. That `stop ~ing` and `stop to ~` are opposite and the trap is well known but still costs
    Korean speakers a sentence at a time.
 4. That `nurse` here is breastfeeding, not care of the sick, and that in this novel the word is
    load-bearing rather than incidental.
