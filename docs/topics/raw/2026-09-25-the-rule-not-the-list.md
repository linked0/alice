WHY MERGE RATHER THAN KEEP FIVE

Each of the five was a list plus a selection rule. Separately, five lists compete for the same slot
in the reader's attention and none of them can be compared with the others. Merged, the comparison
becomes the content: five people answered the same question with five different filters, and the
filters — not the 45 titles — are what transfers.

The merge also surfaced something none of the five could show on its own: the overlaps. Frankenstein
appears in two lists under two different filters, Annie Ernaux appears in two lists with two
different books, and Primo Levi appears in one list and again in the critic's blurb quoted by
another. That is only visible once the lists sit in one card, and it is the card's most useful
derived recommendation.

WHAT THE MERGE COST, recorded honestly

The five originals carried more per-book detail than this card does — full timestamp ranges into
each interview, longer readings of individual books, and per-card Jayverse landings. That detail is
in the raw files. If any single list needs to be a card again it should be split back out rather
than expanded here.

NOT VERIFIED, carried forward from the originals: vote counts, sales and print-run claims, interview
lengths, and all timestamps. The park-hyejin and jung-youngsu cards recorded a 2026-09-19
verification pass on the existence of every book and author named; that pass is not repeated here.
