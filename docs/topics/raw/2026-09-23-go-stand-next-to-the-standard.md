Patrick Collison's advice to people in their 20s: find where the highest standards are, and go
experience them firsthand.

"When I talk to people in other domains, this is so frequently the thing that I hear from them."

"That when they worked with X person or Y organization or in Z environment, they learned what great
actually is, and that just permanently changed their sense for what their own standard for their
work ought to be."

"Maybe one version of what people in their twenties should do is... figure out, where can you learn
the highest standards? Where are the highest standards embodied, and where can you go and experience
that firsthand?"

The card's own arguments, labelled as such in the body:
 1. That the claim is about CALIBRATION, not inspiration — the thing acquired is a reference point,
    and a reference point cannot be described, only met.
 2. That it explains a specific failure that has no other good explanation: people who work hard
    and improve steadily and still plateau, because their target was set by their surroundings.
 3. That "firsthand" is load-bearing and gets skipped. Reading about a high standard transfers the
    description; working next to one transfers the felt gap. Those are different acquisitions.
 4. That the advice has an uncomfortable corollary Collison does not state: if a standard is
    acquired by proximity, then staying somewhere that does not have one is itself a decision about
    your ceiling, and the cost is invisible because you cannot see what you are not being exposed to.
 5. That it is not only for people in their twenties; the twenties are when the cost of moving is
    lowest, which is a different claim from the one the framing implies.
