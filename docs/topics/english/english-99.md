# 99 · Line — Those are the types you have to watch out for.
title_ko: Those are the types you have to watch out for. — 바로 저런 부류를 조심해야 해 (watch out for + 대상)
situation: Yusuf, a friend from Jay's old team in Dubai, is visiting Seoul for the first time. Near Itaewon station a tout in a suit tries to steer them into a "members only" bar, and Jay explains who to be careful of.
situation_ko: 두바이 시절 옛 팀 친구 Yusuf가 서울에 처음 왔다. 이태원역 근처에서 정장 차림의 호객꾼이 "회원 전용" 바로 데려가려 하고, Jay는 누굴 조심해야 하는지 설명한다.
why: **Watch out for** takes an object: watch out for scammers, watch out for that step. Bare "watch out!" is a shout with no object, "danger, now". The drama line moves the object to the front, "those types", and leaves the "for" behind at the end of the clause; that stranded preposition is correct English, and dropping it changes the meaning to "the types you must watch". "The types" is people of a certain kind, and it is slightly dismissive, fine for touts, unkind for colleagues.

## Dialogue
Yusuf: That guy in the suit seemed friendly. Free first drink, he said.
> 정장 입은 그 사람 친절해 보이던데. 첫 잔 무료래.
Jay: Those are the types you have to watch out for. Free first drink, then a bill for the bottle you never ordered.
> 바로 저런 부류를 조심해야 해. 첫 잔은 공짜, 그다음엔 시킨 적 없는 병값이 청구되지.
Yusuf: Watch out, or watch out for? You said both today.
> watch out이야, watch out for야? 오늘 둘 다 말했잖아.
Jay: "Watch out!" alone is a shout, like when you nearly walked into the scooter. "Watch out for" needs a target: watch out for touts, watch out for the step.
> "Watch out!" 하나만은 외침이야, 아까 스쿠터에 부딪힐 뻔했을 때처럼. "Watch out for"는 대상이 필요해. 호객꾼을 조심해, 계단 조심해.
Yusuf: And the "for" at the end of your sentence?
> 그럼 네 문장 끝의 "for"는?
Jay: It stays. I moved "those types" to the front, so the "for" is left waiting at the end.
> 그대로 남아. "those types"를 앞으로 옮겼으니까 "for"가 끝에서 기다리는 거야.

## Techniques
1. **경고는 대상과 수법을 한 문장에 담는다.** "Those are the types you have to watch out for. Free first drink, then a bill…" — 누구를, 왜 조심해야 하는지가 같이 나온다.
2. **문법 질문에는 방금 겪은 일로 대비를 만든다.** "'Watch out!' alone is a shout, like when you nearly walked into the scooter." — 목적어 유무의 차이가 체험으로 기억된다.
