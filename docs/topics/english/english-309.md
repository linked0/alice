# 309 · Line — I'd better fill up with petrol at the next station.

situation: Driving a rental car back to the airport in Britain, Jay checks the agreement and sees the tank must be returned full. The needle is close to the line and the next station is fifteen minutes away.
situation_ko: 영국에서 렌터카를 몰고 공항으로 돌아가는 길에, Jay는 계약서를 보고 연료를 가득 채워 반납해야 한다는 조항을 발견한다. 계기판 바늘은 눈금에 가깝고 다음 주유소는 15분 거리다.
why: **Fill up (with petrol)** is the ordinary phrase for filling a tank, and it works alone: "I need to fill up." The noun is a fill-up. **Petrol** is British; Americans say gas, and the building is a petrol station in Britain and a gas station in the States. **I'd better** is not a plain "should": it means that if you don't, something bad follows — here, the rental company's refuelling fee.

## Dialogue
Nora: The rental agreement says bring it back full.
> 렌터카 계약서에 가득 채워서 반납하라고 되어 있어.
Jay: Then I'd better fill up with petrol at the next station. The needle is almost on the line.
> 그럼 다음 주유소에서 기름을 가득 채워야겠다. 바늘이 거의 눈금에 닿았어.
Nora: In the States you'd say gas.
> 미국이었으면 gas라고 했겠지.
Jay: Right — gas station there, petrol station here. Same pump, two words.
> 맞아. 거긴 gas station, 여긴 petrol station. 같은 주유기에 단어만 둘이지.
Nora: Does "fill up" work on its own? And why "I'd better" rather than "I should"?
> "fill up"만 써도 돼? 그리고 왜 "I should"가 아니라 "I'd better"야?
Jay: It works alone — "I need to fill up", and the noun is a fill-up. I'd better is for when there's a penalty: if I don't, they charge me their refuelling fee.
> 단독으로도 써. "I need to fill up." 명사형은 a fill-up이고. I'd better는 벌칙이 있을 때 쓰는 말이야. 안 채우면 주유 수수료를 물리거든.

## Techniques
1. **영국식과 미국식 어휘는 쌍으로 묶어 둔다.** petrol station ↔ gas station, petrol ↔ gas.
2. **had better는 결과가 붙은 조동사다.** "I'd better is for when there's a penalty" — should와 달리 안 했을 때의 불이익이 전제된다.
