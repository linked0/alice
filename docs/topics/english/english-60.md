why: **Judging by** (or judging from) X opens a conclusion drawn from evidence: "Judging by last time, …" Then get along with means to have a friendly working relationship, and someone's lot is a British, slightly dismissive word for a group of people who belong together, a crew or a gang: "him and his lot". The trap: this lot has nothing to do with "a lot of", and "doesn't seem to" softens the verdict, which is why the line works as bar talk rather than a formal complaint.

## Dialogue
Aoife: Should we put Dan in the platform team's planning session on Thursday?
> 목요일 플랫폼 팀 플래닝에 댄을 넣을까?
Jay: Judging by last time, he doesn't seem to get along with Mark's lot. He argued with them for twenty minutes over one schema field.
> 지난번을 보면 마크네 패거리랑 잘 안 맞는 것 같아. 스키마 필드 하나 갖고 그 사람들이랑 20분을 싸웠잖아.
Aoife: "Mark's lot"? You've been sitting next to Tom for too long.
> "마크네 패거리"? 톰 옆에 너무 오래 앉아 있었구나.
Jay: Probably. I only mean the group, not that they're a gang. But judging by that meeting, I'd rather send Priya and brief Dan afterwards.
> 그런가 봐. 그냥 그 그룹이란 뜻이지, 무슨 일당이란 건 아니야. 그래도 그 회의로 미루어 보면 프리야를 보내고 댄한텐 나중에 브리핑하는 게 낫겠어.
Aoife: Fair. Let's not judge him on one meeting forever, though.
> 알았어. 그래도 회의 한 번으로 계속 평가하진 말자.
Jay: Agreed. If they get along next time, he's in.
> 동의. 다음에 잘 지내면 넣는 걸로.

## Techniques
1. **판단의 근거를 먼저 말한다.** "Judging by last time, he doesn't seem to…" — 결론 앞에 근거를 두고 "doesn't seem to"로 단정을 피하면 험담이 아니라 관찰이 된다.
2. **표현이 세게 들리면 바로 범위를 줄인다.** "I only mean the group, not that they're a gang." — lot이 못마땅한 뉘앙스를 띠므로 상대가 되짚으면 한 문장으로 정정한다.
