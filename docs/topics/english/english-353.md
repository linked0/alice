why: **Rapacious** means greedy to the point of taking by force, and **loot** is the verb for stealing during a war or a riot. Both are strong, written words: a bank that charges high fees can be called rapacious in an editorial, but not in a meeting. Note "loot" is also a noun for the stolen goods, and in games it means the drops you pick up.

## Dialogue
Sam: That whole gallery is marked "provenance disputed". Half of it left the country in 1905.
> 저 전시실 전체가 "출처 분쟁"으로 표시돼 있어. 절반은 1905년에 나라 밖으로 나간 거야.
Jay: The narrator put it plainly: rapacious soldiers looted the houses in the defeated city.
> 내레이터가 대놓고 말하더라. 탐욕스러운 군인들이 함락된 도시의 집들을 약탈했다고.
Sam: Museums rarely use that verb about themselves.
> 박물관은 자기 얘기에 그 동사를 잘 안 쓰지.
Jay: No. They say "acquired". "Looted" names the war, and "rapacious" names the greed.
> 안 쓰지. "acquired"라고 하지. looted는 전쟁을 가리키고, rapacious는 탐욕을 가리키는 말이잖아.
Sam: Could you use rapacious about a company?
> 회사에 대고 rapacious를 쓸 수도 있어?
Jay: In an opinion piece, yes. In a meeting it would sound like a speech, so I'd say "predatory fees" instead.
> 사설에서라면. 회의에서 쓰면 연설처럼 들려서, 나라면 "predatory fees"라고 하겠어.

## Techniques
1. **강한 단어는 "어디서 쓸 수 있나"까지 같이 익힌다.** "In an opinion piece, yes. In a meeting it would sound like a speech." — 뜻과 사용 장소를 한 쌍으로 외운다.
2. **완곡어와 직설어를 나란히 놓으면 뜻이 또렷해진다.** 박물관의 "acquired"와 다큐멘터리의 "looted"가 같은 사건의 두 표현이다.
